Reel audio audit

The Dylan Patel remake of the LeBron "How it go" edit, measured against the original with the same rulers. They cover loudness, band balance, stereo, the vocal stem's content and the two voice lines. Four candidate fixes are stacked one on another, and each is muxed onto the locked 30 fps master for listening in context.

Findings

  • The current mix is 0.7 LU quieter than the original overall (integrated -11.05 against -10.37 LUFS) and 0.9 to 2.4 LU quieter in every section measured. Its short-term loudness sits 0.76 LU off the original on average. The loss comes from three places, listed next.
  • The bed is four stems summed, and the stems do not add up to the track. Original minus the stem sum leaves a residual 19 dB below the mix that peaks in the low-mids and mids (only 15 dB down between 250 Hz and 2 kHz). It is missing from every second of the current mix, worst in the pre-drop at 9 to 10 s, where the current mix is 3 dB down. This is the main source of lost fullness, and it has nothing to do with the announcer.
  • Zeal's suspicion is right. The mute removes far more than the announcer. The vocal stem carries the song's own held note under the intro line, the sung hook under the outro yell, the stereo width and reverb of both, and the edges of the rap after the yell. The mutes run 1.15 to 2.95 s and 23.6 to 26.4 s and take all of it. Under the intro line the music is 3.7 LU quieter than in the original (-17.3 against -13.6 LUFS), partly from the mute and partly from the 2 dB duck.
  • The current final goes silent at 28.978 s. The last 1.09 s, where the original still plays the rap at -26 dB, is digital zero. The cause is the ffmpeg graph in blend.sh and the final render. The voice is split into both the sidechain key and the amix, and ffmpeg ends the ducked bed early. It is a race, not a fixed cut. The same command ended at 29.269 s at 48 kHz and ran to the end in two other runs. Padding the graph inputs fixes it.
  • The limiter costs loudness exactly where the reel is densest. The original's peaks are clipped flat at +0.9 dBTP. The current limiter (limit 0.84, 40 ms release) pulls the newspaper and fast-cut sections down about 0.8 LU. A 10 ms release at the same -1 dBTP-safe ceiling keeps the original's short-term contour within 0.2 LU and changes the signal less (limiter difference -22.5 dB against -18.2 dB).
  • Zeal's intro line sits on top of the music, where the announcer sat inside it. The original announcer runs 6.4 LU under the music around it. The current "Patel" line runs 0.35 LU under it, so it is 6 LU more forward. The outro goes the other way. "DYLAN with the gigawatt" sits 7.4 LU under the music, 1.4 LU further back than the original yell.
  • The voice EQ and space do not match the announcer. The broadcast chain low-passes Zeal's voice at 6.8 kHz, so it has 10 dB less air around 8 kHz than the announcer and more 250 Hz and 700 Hz to 1.4 kHz honk. The announcer is dry and choppy (27.6 dB between the loud and quiet 20 ms frames of the line); the 45 and 95 ms slap on Zeal's line smears it to 17.1 dB.
  • Candidate C is the closest match. It plays the original's own audio wherever the announcer is silent, takes out only the announcer's words, rebuilds both lines dry and EQ'd to the announcer at the announcer's level against the music, and masters to the original's -10.37 LUFS with a -1.39 dBTP peak. Short-term loudness tracks the original within 0.18 LU on average, every band is within 0.1 dB, and every section within 0.4 LU.

What the vocal stem holds, and what the current bed drops

How the current bed was made (audio/compose.sh v2, no-announcer-v2.wav): drums, bass and other from htdemucs_ft summed with the vocal stem, the vocal stem muted from 1.15 to 2.95 s and 23.6 to 26.4 s with 60 ms linear ramps, both channels. The final (mix-final-patel3-5-outro3.wav) is that bed ducked by a sidechain keyed on the intro line (threshold 0.05, ratio 3, mix 0.6), plus memo 3 take 5 through the broadcast chain at +2 dB with 3 dB off under 300 Hz and 2 dB at 3 kHz, plus outro take 3 placed so "Dylan" lands at 24.16 s. Evidence for each row comes from Whisper medium.en words on the stem's mid and side, L/R correlation, level and spectrogram (vocals.py, vocals.json).

Candidates

  • A. Full bed. The original's own audio wherever the announcer is silent, so the stem residual is back. Under the words it is the stems with the song-only vocal stem. Voices, duck and limiter as today, with the graph padded so it runs to the end.
  • B. A plus matched voices. Both lines rebuilt from the raw memos without the 6.8 kHz low-pass or the slap, EQ'd with a linear-phase filter onto the 1/3-octave spectrum of the announcer each replaces (within 8 dB), and set to that announcer's level against the music, LU for the intro and LU for the outro. No duck, because the original bed already carries whatever ducking the editor did. Today's limiter.
  • C. B plus matched master. Gain into a 10 ms release limiter, solved so the integrated loudness lands on the original's -10.37 LUFS, ceiling -1 dBTP.
  • D. C with both lines 2 dB hotter. For a louder Patel than the original announcer, if C reads too tucked in.
