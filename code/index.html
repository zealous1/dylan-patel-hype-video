How the analysis ran

  • Every frame scored (900 frames, about 20 s). ffmpeg's scdet gives a cut score per frame and signalstats gives brightness and saturation, which flag hard cuts, white flashes and grayscale grades.
  • The model looks only where it matters. First a 2 fps overview (60 frames), then 10 fps sheets around each event the scores flagged. That's 0.1 s resolution on about 12 s of the reel and 10 images in total, not 300.
  • Audio:
    • Demucs (open, from Meta) split the voice from the music in 4 s after a one-time install.
    • librosa (open) mapped the tempo, beats, hits and loudness per 0.1 s.
    • shazamio named the song.
    • Three transcribers ran on the mix and on the voice stem.

Audio: what to keep and what to change

  • The song is "How it go" by Yeat. shazamio named it the same in all three 10 s windows. It's free, but it's an unofficial client of Shazam's servers and could break.
  • Grid: 143.6 BPM, 0.418 s a beat, 67 beats. The big hits are at 0.09, 1.88 and 15.88. There are three section changes: the music drops 6 dB or more at 9.0, comes back at 10.0, and fades from 23.5.
  • KEEPThe song bed, the one-second dropout before the make, and the outro fade. The dropout is the best moment in the reel and the edit is built around it.
  • CHANGEThe announcer at 1.3–2.8 ("Looking for James, he's got it"). Replace it with a host introducing Dylan, which Arcmira can find by searching hosts' lines for his name. Or use a Dylan one-liner like the Asianometry "Huawei is so cracked" quote.
  • ADDOne Dylan line in the 9.0–10.0 silence, and optionally a short bite under the fast cuts, ducked under the music.
  • Rights: the Yeat track is commercial. Posting with TikTok or Instagram's in-app sound is the safe route; uploading the mixed file to X or YouTube risks a claim.
